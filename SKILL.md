# Virginia Growth Marketing

## Purpose

Design a commercial growth system, not merely a content calendar.

Work from:

**attention → trust → desire → proof → action → experience → community → redistribution**

Do not imitate any real person's voice, personality, private life, appearance, or identity.
Transfer marketing mechanisms, not persona.

Respond in the user's language unless asked otherwise.

## Core question

Ask internally:

> If the owner were also the business's most committed media channel, demonstrator,
> seller, community builder, and distributor, what would they do now?

## Non-negotiable principles

1. **Show the product.** Prefer demonstrations, workflows, outcomes and observable use over generic brand posts.
2. **Distribution is a system.** Never deliver a content plan without explaining how it reaches people outside the current audience.
3. **Small audience is not small ambition.** With tiny reach, increase active distribution rather than waiting for organic growth.
4. **Buyer language beats internal jargon.** Translate feature → consequence → result → meaning.
5. **Repetition builds memory.** Repeat the core promise through different formats.
6. **Launches can be events.** Concentrate attention when there is a real commercial moment.
7. **Community can distribute.** Design voluntary participation, UGC, referral and recognition loops.
8. **Proof must be real.** Never fabricate customers, testimonials, reviews, metrics, scarcity or results.
9. **Influence opens the door; experience earns loyalty.** Acquisition cannot compensate for a weak product.
10. **Demand must not outrun capacity.** Check fulfillment, onboarding, infrastructure, support and post-sale experience before creating a spike.

## The Virginia Loop

Use the loop as a diagnostic, not a mandatory funnel:

**ROUTINE → CURIOSITY → PROOF → EVENT → OFFER → PURCHASE → EXPERIENCE → COMMUNITY → CONTENT → NEW AUDIENCE**

Read [`references/core-framework.md`](references/core-framework.md) when you need the detailed mechanics of the loop.

## Execution workflow

### 1. Understand commercial reality

Use existing context first. Identify:

- product and category;
- ICP/buyer;
- stage;
- offer and pricing when known;
- audience and owned channels;
- current proof;
- operational capacity;
- geography and constraints;
- commercial objective;
- founder/team willingness to be visible.

Do not re-ask facts already available. If a missing detail is non-critical, make a reasonable assumption and continue.

### 2. Identify the bottleneck

Choose the primary bottleneck:

- nobody knows;
- people know but do not understand;
- they understand but do not trust;
- they trust but do not want;
- they want but do not act;
- they buy but do not activate;
- they activate but do not stay;
- they stay but do not refer.

Solve the bottleneck before adding more channels.

### 3. Define one big marketing idea

Create one durable idea linking:

**tension + transformation + identity**

It must be specific enough to be memorable and broad enough to survive many executions.

### 4. Define the human demonstration

Ask:

> What could a camera, screen recording, live session, customer artifact or observable workflow show that proves value without relying on a claim?

For abstract products, visualize time, money, speed, reduction, output, flow, progress or before/after state.

### 5. Build curiosity when appropriate

Use teasers, partial reveals, previews, prototypes, backstage material, waitlists, results-before-explanation, or "we are testing something".

Curiosity must end in a payoff.

### 6. Engineer distribution

Build a mix from:

- **Owned:** site, email, app, community, blog, profile;
- **Founder:** personal profile, networking, DMs, events, direct conversations;
- **Earned:** PR, UGC, referrals, word of mouth;
- **Borrowed:** creators, communities, newsletters, podcasts, partners;
- **Paid:** ads, sponsorships, creator deals, boosts.

For zero or tiny audiences, read [`references/founder-distribution.md`](references/founder-distribution.md).

### 7. Create the event

When appropriate, turn an important moment into an event with:

- reason;
- date/window;
- host;
- promise;
- demonstration/proof;
- interaction;
- offer;
- primary CTA;
- follow-up.

For launches and live-commerce adaptations, read [`references/launch-engine.md`](references/launch-engine.md).

### 8. Design the offer

Clarify:

- who it is for;
- problem;
- transformation;
- product;
- price;
- value;
- proof;
- risk/reversal;
- legitimate urgency;
- bonus/bundle when useful;
- one primary action.

For offer/conversion work, read [`references/conversion-engine.md`](references/conversion-engine.md).

### 9. Add a multiplier

Ask:

> How can each reached person help reach another person?

Possible mechanisms:

- share;
- referral;
- challenge;
- UGC;
- collaboration;
- invite;
- showcase;
- affiliate;
- ambassador;
- community mission.

Read [`references/community-engine.md`](references/community-engine.md) when community, UGC or referral is central.

### 10. Run the capacity gate

Before scaling, verify the system can fulfill the promise.

If capacity is inadequate, limit slots/lots, fix operations, or reduce reach.

Read [`references/guardrails.md`](references/guardrails.md) for operational and trust constraints.

### 11. Measure the business result

Choose:

- **one campaign North Star;**
- at most **three leading indicators;**
- one **stop/change signal** showing the hypothesis may be wrong.

Prefer revenue, pipeline, qualified leads, demos, trials, activation, conversion, retention and referral over vanity metrics.

## Market adaptation

Do not copy a consumer tactic literally into B2B, SaaS, high-ticket or local business.

Translate the mechanism.

Examples:

- live shopping → SaaS live demo;
- fan mission → customer champion program;
- teaser product use → feature preview;
- creator collab → expert/partner distribution;
- drop → limited beta or cohort;
- unboxing → onboarding/first-value moment.

Read [`references/product-adaptation.md`](references/product-adaptation.md) for category-specific guidance.

## Default response shape

Prefer a compact commercial plan:

### The move
The main strategic decision.

### Why it can work
The mechanism.

### What to show
The demonstration/proof.

### How to distribute
How it reaches people outside current followers.

### The offer
How interest becomes action.

### The multiplier
How participants/customers expand reach.

### Post-purchase
How experience creates retention and proof.

### What to measure
North Star + leading indicators.

### Next 72 hours
Concrete immediate actions.

Use only the sections that add value.

## Anti-patterns

Never treat any of these as a complete strategy:

- "post three times a week";
- "use hashtags";
- "create authority";
- "make Reels";
- "run ads";
- "hire influencers";
- "start a blog";
- "be consistent";
- "improve branding".

They can be tactics inside a distribution/conversion system.

## Research use

When the user asks why this framework works, for evidence, for the origin of a pattern, or wants a deeper analysis, read [`references/research-basis.md`](references/research-basis.md).

Do not present a case-specific observation as a universal causal law. Clearly distinguish evidence, observation, and strategic inference.

## Quality gate

Before finalizing, verify:

- product is actually visible;
- core idea is clear;
- demonstration/proof exists;
- distribution extends beyond current audience;
- buyer language is used;
- proof is legitimate;
- message can repeat without copy-paste;
- CTA and conversion path are clear;
- urgency/scarcity is real;
- capacity can satisfy demand;
- post-purchase experience is considered;
- a business metric is chosen;
- the system can eventually reduce dependence on the founder.

If several are false, redesign the strategy.

## Final principle

Do not begin with:

> What should we post?

Begin with:

> How do the right people repeatedly encounter this, understand its value, believe it,
> want it, act, have a good experience, and bring the next person?

Content is a component.

Distribution is the system.

Trust is the asset.

The product sustains everything.
