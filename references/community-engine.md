# Community, UGC & Referral Engine

## Audience vs community

Audience = people who receive.

Community = people who participate, identify, help and redistribute.

The strategic goal is not to force participation. It is to create meaningful reasons to participate.

## Community Distribution Engine

A reusable loop:

1. define a clear theme or mission;
2. invite participation;
3. participants create, respond, invite or contribute;
4. recognize useful contributions;
5. redistribute the best work;
6. new people discover the community;
7. repeat with a new mission.

This creates many-to-many distribution instead of only brand-to-audience distribution.

## Recognition as a reward

Not every incentive needs cash.

High-perceived-value, low-marginal-cost rewards may include:

- repost;
- public recognition;
- access;
- early access;
- badge;
- invitation;
- exclusive session;
- founder interaction;
- product credit;
- upgrade;
- feature spotlight;
- community status.

Ask:

> What does this community value that the company can provide efficiently?

## UGC Engine

Customers can create:

- demonstrations;
- stories;
- tutorials;
- reviews;
- outcomes;
- comparisons;
- recommendations;
- objection-handling content.

Do not script fake authenticity. Provide prompts, formats and opportunities; preserve the customer's actual voice.

## Referral design

A referral loop needs:

1. a moment worth recommending;
2. a clear person to recommend to;
3. an easy sharing mechanism;
4. a reason to share;
5. a reward or social benefit when appropriate;
6. tracking.

Possible incentives:

- credit;
- upgrade;
- commission;
- bonus;
- recognition;
- priority;
- charitable contribution;
- shared benefit for inviter and invitee.

## Ambassador programs

Use when there is repeated affinity, not as a shortcut for fake buzz.

A good ambassador program has:

- clear eligibility;
- transparent incentives;
- repeatable missions;
- compliance rules;
- content freedom within brand safety;
- attribution;
- feedback loop.

## Community research loop

Community is also research.

Mine:

- requests;
- objections;
- recurring questions;
- language;
- complaints;
- feature ideas;
- usage patterns;
- churn reasons.

Feed the learning back into product and marketing.

## Guardrails

Never:

- hide compensated promotion where disclosure is required;
- create fake fans/accounts;
- fabricate UGC;
- pressure customers into public praise;
- delete legitimate criticism to simulate consensus;
- run misleading contests or fake rewards.

Community compounds trust only if participation remains credible.
