# Founder & Distribution

## Founder-led distribution

When a business is small, the founder has assets a corporate account usually lacks:

- face;
- point of view;
- story;
- context;
- credibility;
- access;
- network;
- ability to converse directly;
- ability to receive raw feedback.

Possible founder roles:

1. media channel;
2. seller;
3. demonstrator;
4. host;
5. interviewer;
6. educator;
7. narrator;
8. connector;
9. community builder;
10. feedback receiver.

Founder-led does **not** require exposing private life. Use presence, not uncontrolled personal exposure.

## Audience Zero Protocol

If there is no meaningful audience, do not wait for algorithms.

Use:

- manual outreach;
- networking;
- comments that add value;
- niche communities;
- events;
- contextual DMs;
- email;
- partnerships;
- micro-creators;
- adjacent founders;
- specialists;
- associations;
- potential customers;
- private demos.

Initial objective:

> Make the first 100 right people aware the product exists.

Then:

> Identify which 10 care enough to engage.

Then:

> Learn why 3 would buy.

Early manual work is simultaneously market research, distribution and sales.

## Tiny-audience rule

If the brand has few followers, every strategy should contain at least two distribution mechanisms outside the owned profile.

Examples:

- partner distribution;
- creator collaboration;
- community posting;
- outbound;
- events;
- earned media;
- referrals;
- guest content.

Do not confuse "post more" with "reach more".

## Borrowed Audience Protocol

If you do not own attention, borrow trusted attention.

Potential sources:

- creators;
- newsletters;
- podcasts;
- communities;
- events;
- complementary companies;
- suppliers;
- customers;
- experts;
- associations;
- marketplaces;
- distributors.

Ask:

> Who already has the trust of the people we need to reach?

Trust fit is often more valuable than raw follower count.

## Outbound with inbound quality

Avoid generic spam.

Bad:

> Hi, check out our innovative solution.

Better process:

1. identify a relevant person;
2. understand their context;
3. initiate a useful interaction;
4. give value or insight;
5. show the product when relevant;
6. invite a low-friction next step.

## Founder dependence

Founder-led marketing should create assets that eventually survive the founder.

Build:

- brand recognition;
- product reputation;
- community;
- SEO;
- email;
- CRM;
- referrals;
- partners;
- customer stories;
- independent distribution channels.

Ask periodically:

> If the founder stopped posting for 30 days, would demand continue?

If not, treat founder dependence as a strategic risk.
