# Core Framework

## The loop

**ROUTINE → CURIOSITY → PROOF → EVENT → OFFER → PURCHASE → EXPERIENCE → COMMUNITY → CONTENT → NEW AUDIENCE**

Treat this as a diagnostic loop, not a rigid funnel.

## 1. Routine — normalize the product before selling it

Place the product naturally inside the context where value is created.

For physical products, show use, preparation, routine, comparison and legitimate before/after.

For software, show the interface being used, a real workflow, an automation, a notification, a dashboard, or a measurable result.

For services, show process, method, diagnosis, preparation and transformation.

For B2B, show the operational problem, handoff, workflow, cost avoided, time saved or manual-vs-new-process comparison.

The principle is: **make the product familiar before asking for commitment.**

## 2. Curiosity — make the market ask before you explain everything

Useful devices include:

- teaser;
- preview;
- prototype;
- partial reveal;
- backstage content;
- countdown;
- result before explanation;
- waitlist;
- private access;
- "we are testing something";
- question-driven reveal.

Curiosity must resolve. Avoid indefinite suspense.

## 3. Proof — demonstrate, do not merely claim

Use the strongest proof available:

1. verified customer result;
2. repeat purchase/retention behavior;
3. documented case;
4. authentic testimonial;
5. observable usage;
6. live demonstration;
7. objective comparison;
8. test/prototype;
9. founder explanation.

Without customers, use product proof. Never manufacture social proof.

## 4. Event — concentrate attention

A commercial moment can become an event:

- launch;
- live;
- demo;
- webinar;
- drop;
- cohort;
- beta;
- reveal;
- release;
- partnership;
- promotion;
- milestone;
- benchmark;
- report.

Design **before + during + after**.

Before: anticipation and relevance.

During: synchronized attention, proof, interaction, offer.

After: clips, reactions, proof, follow-up, retargeting, evergreen assets.

## 5. Offer — attention must lead somewhere

Clarify:

- who it is for;
- what pain it solves;
- what outcome it creates;
- why this option;
- why now;
- what proof reduces risk;
- what the user must do next.

Use legitimate urgency only. Scarcity must be true.

## 6. Purchase — minimize friction

Map:

**content → one primary action → confirmation → onboarding**

Possible primary actions:

- buy;
- trial;
- book;
- request demo;
- join waitlist;
- install;
- create account;
- message;
- reserve;
- request quote.

## 7. Experience — marketing continues after payment

Audit:

- delivery;
- onboarding;
- support;
- first-use experience;
- time-to-value;
- expectation match;
- cancellation;
- refund;
- recovery from failure.

A strong experience converts customers into proof and distribution.

## 8. Community — turn audience into participation

Audience watches. Community participates.

Mechanisms:

- missions;
- challenges;
- voting;
- betas;
- feedback;
- rankings;
- showcases;
- referrals;
- ambassador programs;
- private groups;
- early access;
- co-creation.

Participation must be voluntary and transparent.

## 9. Content — turn use into distribution

Ask:

> What naturally shareable moment does the product create?

Examples:

- result;
- milestone;
- dashboard;
- achievement;
- visual transformation;
- report;
- certificate;
- before/after;
- unboxing;
- first win;
- customer story.

If no shareable moment exists, consider designing one into the experience.

## Frequency doctrine

A single exposure rarely creates understanding, trust and action.

Repeat the same core promise through different containers:

- demo;
- video;
- story;
- email;
- FAQ;
- comparison;
- case;
- meme;
- live;
- landing page;
- ad;
- webinar.

Repeat the message. Vary the packaging.

## Human-language rule

Translate:

**feature → consequence → result → meaning**

Example:

Feature: automatic synchronization.

Consequence: no manual updates.

Result: less repetitive work.

Meaning: the user finishes the day without carrying admin work home.

Marketing should reach the meaning.

## Attention ladder

A strong campaign helps people move through:

1. Stop;
2. Recognize;
3. Understand;
4. Believe;
5. Desire;
6. Act;
7. Experience;
8. Continue;
9. Tell;
10. Participate.

If a plan jumps directly from awareness to purchase, identify the missing steps.
